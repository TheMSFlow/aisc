## 2. The readiness dimensions (approved by the founder, 2026-10-08)

The resource the founder shared (`AI Readiness ENG.pdf`) measures an organisation: strategy, data, talent, processes, governance. So do the Cisco and Microsoft readiness indexes. Those do not fit a leader scoring themselves. Research on **individual** readiness points to a different set.

| Source | What it measures in a person |
|---|---|
| MAIRS readiness scale (Karaca et al.) | Cognition, ability, vision, ethics |
| AI Literacy Scale (Wang, Rau, Yuan) | Awareness, usage, evaluation, ethics |
| Meta AI Literacy Scale (MAILS) | Adds self-efficacy and self-management, including not letting AI sway one's decisions |
| Technology Readiness Index (Parasuraman) | Motivators (optimism, innovativeness) against inhibitors (discomfort, insecurity) |
| Readiness for change (Holt et al.) | Belief the change is needed, personal stake, belief one can do it |
| BCG leader findings (2026) | Daily personal use and deliberate experimentation separate leaders of AI-mature organisations |

The six, each covered by at least two of the sources:

| Dimension | The question it answers | Drawn from |
|---|---|---|
| **Vision** | Do I see what AI changes in my field and my role, and why it matters to me now? | MAIRS vision, Holt |
| **Confidence** | Do I approach AI with curiosity, or with discomfort and avoidance? | Technology Readiness Index, MAILS self-efficacy |
| **Understanding** | Can I say plainly what AI is, what it is not, and where it fails? | MAIRS cognition, awareness |
| **Practice** | Do I use AI myself, regularly, and experiment on purpose? | Ability, usage, BCG |
| **Judgment** | Can I tell a good output from a confident wrong one, and keep my own view? | Evaluation, MAILS |
| **Responsibility** | Do I have my own rules for data, disclosure and accountability? | Ethics, in all three scales |

Design rules taken from the research:

- **Score behaviour, not self-opinion.** Leaders over-rate themselves: in a Korn Ferry survey, 78% of leaders said they had AI figured out and 39% of their workers agreed. Each dimension gets three or four statements about what the leader actually did recently, rated 1 to 5.
- **Read the profile, not the total.** The imbalance is the finding.
- **Make no validation claim.** This is a reflection tool informed by published scales, not a psychometric test. No scale items are copied, and nothing from the shared PDF is copied.

What the scorecard shows after scoring:

## 4. Tasks

### Prerequisite task: Readiness Scorecard

- Rate the 24 statements in the tool and download the summary.
- Readiness Scorecard Worksheet, Section A: copy the six scores, and name the strongest dimension and the one to start with.
- Section B: three Readiness Moves, one for where you start, one for the next lowest, one that uses the strongest. Each has a day.
- Retake after the first month in Labs.

### Primary task: Data Readiness Audit

The name says what it is (founder, 2026-10-08): the participant's data is audited to see how it works today and how it can get ready for AI. The steps and tips are in Part 2 of the guide. The findings go on one page of the Data Readiness Audit Worksheet, with a second section for questions that still need an answer.

- **Choose** three sets of data the leader's work depends on, for example people, money, the work.
- **Collected:** where does it come from, who enters it, how often, and how much is typed by hand?
- **Stored:** where does it live, how many copies exist, and who can reach it?
- **Responsible:** who answers for it being right, and who decides what may be shared? "Nobody" is a valid and useful finding.
- **Ready for AI:** four tests (complete, current, in one place, clear to share), then a mark of ready, nearly ready or not ready, and the one step that would move it forward.

It is an audit, not a fix. Nothing is repaired during the challenge, and nothing is put into an AI tool. The first steps it names are carried into Labs.

### Completion check

Each worksheet ends with its own check. The page carries one for the day: the scorecard is taken and its summary downloaded, the scores and three moves are in the Readiness Scorecard Worksheet, three sets of data are audited, and each is marked with one first step.
