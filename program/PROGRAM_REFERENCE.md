## 2. Before Day 1

### Onboarding

**Source:** LC `onboarding/` (welcome, brief, expectations, profile). No documents.

- **Welcome:** a few minutes of setup, how the challenge works, and some context about the participant.
- **Brief:** the framing statement. AI as "the vehicle of this age"; the leader's charge to shepherd people through disruption; untapped ground in every industry; the challenge as "a call to become a positioned stakeholder in the age of AI: a steward of your people, a claimant of your territory."
- **Expectations:** the curriculum schedule for the participant's tier, plus three stances: clarity over perfection, write for yourself, expect discomfort.
- **Profile:** role, industry and context. Later tools depend on it. The Territory Builder will not open without a completed profile.

### Day 0: Pre-Challenge Setup

**Format:** self-paced. **Source:** LC `day-0/` (overview, setup, ready). **No documents exist for Day 0.**

**Objectives** *(draft)*
- Remove avoidable friction before the first live session.
- Have a paid AI tool working, so Day 2 is not spent on access problems.
- Start the two lists the rest of the challenge draws on.

**What participants do.** Four checks, all required to continue:
1. **Primary AI access:** Claude Pro (recommended) or ChatGPT Go, Plus or Pro. The page states that free tiers are not sufficient.
2. **Focus folder:** one folder on their computer named "AI Stakeholder".
3. **Friction log:** start capturing repeated frustrations and slowdowns, without solving them.
4. **Recurring tasks:** a separate list of what repeats in daily work.

The page asks for a computer or laptop rather than a phone.

**Outputs:** a working AI tool, the folder, the friction log (used on Day 3), the recurring-task list (used on Day 1).

**Closing frame (Ready page):** over seven days the participant will decide what deserves their attention, what no longer does, and where to direct their leadership energy.

## 3. The three core days

### Day 1: Mindset, Meaning & Mechanics

| | |
|---|---|
| **Live session** | AI Clarity, 2 hrs, all tiers |
| **Learning center title** | "AI Clarity & Leadership" |
| **Pillar** | AI Fluency (Clarity), opening AI Leadership |
| **4D focus** | D1 Delegation, D2 Description |

**Objectives** *(draft)*
1. Shift the leadership frame from doing the work to designing how work gets done, by humans and AI.
2. Hold a plain-language picture of what AI is: patterns, and "digital labour" that performs thinking work.
3. Know the three modes of interaction and the 4D framework well enough to name them.
4. Sort real, recurring tasks into AUTOMATE, AUGMENT, STREAMLINE and OWN, and commit to one concrete 30-day action per quadrant.

**What the session teaches** (Docs: `day-1/day-1-session-notes.md`, 12 slides, three parts)

- **Part 1, Mindset.** "AI is not changing what leaders know. It is changing what leaders are responsible for." Leader versus AI Leader. The three mandatory shifts: Control to Leverage, Knowledge to Judgment, Doing to Delegating.
- **Part 2, Meaning.** AI in one word: patterns. Software speeds up work, automation repeats work, AI performs thinking work. "See it as Digital Labour."
- **Part 3, Mechanics.** What humans provide (critical thinking, judgment, creativity, ethical oversight) and what AI provides (speed, scale, pattern recognition, processing power). The three modes: Automation, Augmentation, Agency. The 4D framework, with AI Fluency defined as interacting with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical and safe.
  - **D1 Delegation:** problem awareness, platform awareness, task delegation.
  - **D2 Description:** product description (what), process description (how), performance description (how AI should behave).
- **Closing reflection:** "What is the ONE thing you will do differently tomorrow?"

**Task: Delegation Audit** (LC `day-1/page.jsx`, 30 minutes)

- Section A, My Task List: name the tasks to evaluate, drawn from the last two weeks.
- Section B, Michael Steve's Delegation Matrix: rate each task on AI Capability Fit and Human Intelligence Demand in the Delegation Tool, then download the delegation summary.
- Section C, My Action Plan: in the Delegation Worksheet, one concrete action per task for the next 30 days.
- **Completion check:** at least 3 tasks rated, summary downloaded, at least one concrete action per quadrant represented.

**Outputs:** delegation summary (download from the tool), completed Delegation Worksheet, an identified AUGMENT task that Day 2 depends on.

### Day 2: Margins

| | |
|---|---|
| **Live session** | AI Labs, 2 hrs, all tiers |
| **Learning center title** | "Reclaiming Leadership Capacity" |
| **Pillar** | AI Leadership, with Fluency in practice |
| **4D focus** | D2 Description (advanced), D3 Discernment, D4 Diligence (safety) |
| **Mode** | Augmentation |

**Objectives** *(draft)*
1. Tell judgment work from attention work, and name where judgment is leaking.
2. Run the Description-Discernment Loop on a real AUGMENT task until the output can be trusted.
3. Put a number on the capacity reclaimed, per task, per week, per quarter and per year.
4. Work within the safety rules for automation, augmentation and data.

**What the session teaches** (Docs: `day-2/day-2-session-notes.md`, 16 slides, three parts)

- **Part 1, Margins: Protect Your Judgment Capacity.** Judgment work versus attention work. Three ways judgment gets drained: false urgency, judgment leakage, reactive leadership. The Margin Principle. The diagnostic question ("If a capable person had clear instructions, could they do 80% of this?") and the Deconstruction Method (Input, Thinking, Output).
- **Part 2, Method: The Description-Discernment Loop.** Brief, Output, Discernment, Refine. "Adjust the brief, not AI." Co-producer, not quality control. Six advanced description techniques (Redirect Mid-Loop, Narrow the Frame, Set the Contrast, Layer the Thinking, Use Your Own Voice, Ask What Is Missing). Four discernment questions (Is it accurate? Is it complete? Is my judgment preserved? What still needs a human decision?).
- **Part 3, Multiplier: The Math of Reclaimed Capacity.** Augmentation converts time rather than saving it. The Margin Multiplier: margin per task, times weekly frequency, times 13 for the quarter, times 52 for the year. Worked examples, up to 156 hours a year for research synthesis.
- **Closing reflection:** "Name one task you will augment this week, and estimate the margin it will create."

**Task: Redeem Your Time** (LC `day-2/page.jsx`, 1 hour)

1. Open the Delegation Worksheet and pick one AUGMENT task.
2. Read the Augmentation Toolkit.
3. Run the Description-Discernment Loop on it in Claude or ChatGPT.
4. Log the time saved in the Margin Calculator.

- Section A, My Augmentation Task: the task, what a good outcome looks like, the non-negotiables.
- Section B, My Brief: Role, Context, Deliverable, optional examples or constraints.
- Section C, Margin Calculation: time without AI versus with AI, and how often it recurs.
- **Completion check:** a final output trusted enough to act on, and the task logged with its annual saving visible.

**Outputs:** one trusted AI-assisted output, a logged margin figure. Both become the Roadmap's "Proof of Leverage".

### Day 3: Money

| | |
|---|---|
| **Live session** | AI Value, 2 hrs, all tiers |
| **Learning center title** | "Ownership & Value Control" |
| **Pillar** | AI Value |
| **Mode** | Agency, as a horizon rather than a task |

**Objectives** *(draft)*
1. Accept the mandate: the standard for AI use in a leader's domain is theirs to set, and so is the accountability.
2. See value as relief from tolerated friction, and place friction in the Eroding, Shifting or Emerging zone.
3. Choose one Competitive Position and one friction point to claim.
4. Write a Declaration Statement that is defensible today.
5. Understand Agency well enough to know what has to be built before it is viable.

**What the session teaches** (Docs: `day-3/day-3-session-notes.md`, 20 slides, three parts)

- **Part 1, Mandate: Lead in an AI-Shaped World.** "A mandate is not an opportunity. It exists whether you accept it or not." Three dimensions: ethics and governance, diligence, education and mentorship. The five ethical commitments: transparency, fairness, accountability, workforce responsibility, integrity.
- **Part 2, Money: See Where Value Actually Lives.** Value comes from relief, not ideas. The clearest signal of friction is "That's just how it works." The three value zones: Eroding (AI is eating this), Shifting (value is moving to new players), Emerging (AI is creating this), each with signals and a move.
- **Part 3, Map: Claim Your Territory.** The five Competitive Positions, one slide each with "best for", what the position does, examples and a question. "Completing the Trilogy": Automate, Augment, Agency. Six prerequisites for Agency.
- **Closing reflection:** "Where will your judgment be invested for the next six months?"

**Tasks** (LC `day-3/page.jsx`)

- **Prerequisite task: AI Value Map** (30 minutes). Read it before using the tool, to understand the zones and positions.
- **Milestone task: Territory Declaration** (15 minutes).

  - Section B, Transfer to Declaration Template: download the PDF summary and transfer it into the Territory Declaration document.
- **Completion check:** 3 to 10 friction points scored and ranked; one claimed with prompts answered; statement saved with "Save Declaration"; work transferred to the template.

**Outputs:** friction scorecard, Competitive Position, Declaration Statement (which goes on the Certificate of Declaration), completed Territory Declaration document (which is what Days 4 and 5 review).

## 4. Days 4 to 7

### Days 4 and 5: Milestone reflection and review

**Format:** self-paced, with the track set by tier. **Source:** LC `peer-reflection/`, `milestone-review/`, `PeerReflection.jsx`, `ProjectReview.jsx`. **No documents.**

The object under review on both tracks is the **Territory Declaration** from Day 3.

**Objectives** *(draft)*
- Sharpen the territory declaration before it is committed to a six-month plan.
- GA: learn to recognise clarity and noise in work the participant is not attached to.
- VIP and VVIP: get direct strategic feedback and alignment from the facilitator.

**General Admission: Peer Reflection** (optional, "highly recommended")
- Agree to the reflection rules: observation, not evaluation; no advice or solutions; respond only to the prompts.
- Share the Territory Declaration as a view-only Google Doc link, with no name or identifiers. One submission only.
- Reflect on three anonymised submissions. Reflections on the participant's own work unlock only after that.
- The three prompts: *What feels clear and decisive here? What single question would help this person sharpen their focus? What feels unnecessary or distracting?*

**VIP and VVIP: Milestone Review**
- Submit a view-only link to the Territory Declaration. Up to three drafts, each receiving facilitator feedback.
- Book a 30-minute one-to-one strategy session before the engagement ends.
- **Day 5:** 1-hour live Q&A session.

VIP participants never see the Peer Reflection page. Both pages open and close on the review window set in the timeline.

### Day 6: 6-Month Roadmap

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | GA: instructional video, self-paced. VIP and VVIP: 2-hour deep dive with Q&A follow-up |
| **Source** | LC `roadmap/page.jsx`; Docs `6-month-roadmap/` |

**Objectives** *(draft)*
1. Assess honestly where the participant stands against what their territory requires.
2. Map the people the next six months depend on.
3. Set personal standards for AI use.
4. Turn the Day 3 declaration into a three-phase, 180-day commitment with named milestones, and start it within seven days.

**What participants do**

*Complete before building the Roadmap* (the three prerequisite documents):
1. **The Capability Gap Audit:** self-rating, 1 to 5, across foundation, domain and market, leadership and influence, and network and ecosystem capabilities, plus position-specific "Must Have Now" requirements. Feeds the Roadmap's Constraints section and Phase 1.
2. **The AI Stakeholder Identifier:** four stakeholder categories (internal, industry, ecosystem, governance), stakeholder profiles, a priority matrix (Activate, Convert, Nurture, Monitor), position-specific guidance, and a top-ten engagement plan. Feeds Phase 2 and Constraints.
3. **My Personal AI Policy:** six personal standards and a signed commitment. Feeds the Executive Summary.

*Then the Roadmap itself,* in five sections that build in order:
1. **Executive Summary:** the Personal Mandate, position and territory at a glance, horizon statement, why now.
2. **Proof of Leverage:** the Day 1 and Day 2 numbers, before and after.
3. **Territory Declaration:** refined from Day 3, closing with the statement "I am a [position] who helps [who] solve [friction] by [how], so that [outcome]."
4. **The 6-Month Roadmap:** three phases, each with a focus by position, four outcome milestones, a Daily Win Pattern and key activities.

   | Phase | Days | Theme |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | 1 to 60 | Clarity & Labs |
   | 2 | 31 to 120 | Implementation & Relationships |
   | 3 | 91 to 180 | Governance & Mandate |

5. **Constraints & Reality Check:** five named constraints with a response to each, the support needed, and a protocol for the momentum drops around Day 45 and Day 100.

Closes with the **Activation Sprint**, seven specific actions for the first seven days with at least two done the same day, and the review cadence at 30, 90 and 180 days.

- **Completion check:** a clear six-month direction; knowing what is in focus and what is not; the Roadmap can guide real decisions immediately.

The Roadmap page unlocks on Day 6 and stays open permanently afterwards.

## 6. Findings: where the program disagrees with itself

Found while compiling this report. Listed because edits and moves are coming, and each of these is a place where moving one thing leaves another pointing at nothing. None has been changed.

### Structure

1. **Day 7 has no content on record, and its subject is already taught on Day 3.** Day 7 is "The Mandate", a live session on AI Governance.
