- **A Convener is added.** "The convener builds relationships and community. It is a great fit because many Chiefs and senior leaders would not have the time to build, but they can rally around an idea."

- **The AI Guardian Challenge** (the founder, 2026-10-23): a challenge for institutional leaders, not yet built, which will run alongside AISC on some days. In its coaching, the preferred position is the governance position, then the Educator. He asked whether that is reason enough to name the position "Guardian" rather than "Governor".

## Decided: Guardian, not Governor

**Decided 2026-10-23:** "Yes, name it Guardian." The reasons put to him: the new challenge alone would be a fair reason; together with the second point below, it settles it.

1. **It names the leaders the position is for after the programme built for them.** An AI Guardian Challenge participant who declares "I am a Guardian who helps…" carries one identity from the challenge into the coaching.
2. **"Governor" is an office in Nigeria.** It is the title of a state's elected head, so "I am a Governor who helps [who] solve [friction]…" in a Declaration Statement reads as a claim to office. A commissioner, a bishop or a vice chancellor can call themselves a Guardian without claiming anything they do not hold.
3. **It fits how religious and institutional leaders see their role:** protecting the people in their care.

What "Governor" had that "Guardian" must keep: authority, and the link to Day 7's "AI Governance". The risk with "Guardian" is that it reads as defensive, the one who blocks AI. The definition below answers that: its product is trust, and good standards are what let AI spread safely.

The drafts below use **Guardian**. The definitions themselves are still with the founder for review.

## The six, by a leader's three duties

The brief's core premise (`agent-guides/AISC_BRIEF.md`): every leader is tasked with Direction, People and Execution. The six give each duty two positions.

| Duty | Position | Creates value by |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction:** where things go | Producer | building what comes next |
| | Advisor | judgment and trusted intelligence others act on |
| **People:** who does the work, how they grow | Educator | building capability |
| | Convener | building relationships and community around an idea |
| **Execution:** systems, processes, standards | Optimizer | improving the systems |
| | Guardian | setting the standards |

## Draft: the AI Value Map, Part 2

Written in the document's own form (tagline, best for, what this position does, examples, your question). The founder's sentences are kept wherever they still hold. Each position says what was kept, changed or added.

### The opening paragraph

> Seeing friction is necessary but not sufficient. You also need to know how you will act on what you see. The six positions below represent the six ways leaders create value with AI, in the organisation or community they lead or in the wider market. Read all six before you choose one.

*Changed:* "five ways leaders create value in an AI-shaped market" became the six ways, in the leader's own world or in the market.

### Added to the "How to choose your position" box, after its two sentences

> Two questions separate the closest pairs. Do people come to you for your judgment, or to learn from you? That is the Advisor or the Educator. Do you hold the authority to decide, or the standing to gather? That is the Guardian or the Convener.

### 1. Optimizer

*Make what exists work significantly better*

**Best for:** Leaders with deep operational knowledge of an existing system. Your advantage is knowing where the real inefficiencies live. Something an outsider cannot replicate quickly.

**What this position does:** The Optimizer improves an existing system from within. They use AI to reduce cost, increase speed, eliminate error, or strengthen margins inside an organisation or industry they already know well. No new product required, just a sharper version of what is already there.

**Examples:**

- Redesigning internal workflows to reduce manual processing time
- Improving accuracy and speed in compliance, reporting, or quality control
- Using AI to do at scale what previously required significant headcount

**Your question:** *Where in my domain is there a persistent inefficiency that AI could systematically eliminate?*

*Unchanged.* It already reads as a stake in the leader's own world.

### 2. Producer

*Build something people need that does not exist yet*

**Best for:** Leaders who can see a gap clearly enough to build toward it. Your advantage is the combination of domain knowledge and willingness to create something that does not yet exist.

**What this position does:** The Producer creates a new product, service, or tool that solves a real friction point others feel, whether for a market or for the people their own organisation or community serves. The Producer does not need to build everything. They take responsibility for creating the value, whether through technology, service design, or intellectual property.

**Examples:**

- Developing an AI-assisted service that solves a chronic industry problem
- Launching a new AI-assisted service for the people your organisation, ministry or institution serves
- Creating a product that replaces a manual process others still accept as normal
- Packaging expertise and AI capability into a scalable offer

**Your question:** *What friction, in my industry or among the people I serve, is costly enough to deserve a better solution, and am I positioned to build it?*

*Changed:* the tagline (it was "Build something the market needs and will pay for"), "others in the industry feel" became "others feel, whether for a market or for the people their own organisation or community serves", and the question no longer asks only what people would pay for. *Added:* the second example.

### 3. Advisor (the Curator is merged into it)

*Give judgment, direction, and clarity others can act on*

**Best for:** Leaders whose value is in perspective, experience, discernment and the ability to synthesise. Your advantage is the credibility that comes from having been in the room when similar decisions were made, and the reliability of your signal in a very noisy space.

**What this position does:** The Advisor does not build tools or redesign systems. They guide the people who look to them, whether a board, a congregation, an audience or clients, through AI decisions. Their product is judgment. The ability to help others navigate complexity, avoid costly mistakes, and move with clarity through a landscape others find confusing. Some advise one to one. Others publish, and their judgment reaches many as trusted intelligence: they research, evaluate and synthesise what is happening into something others can act on.

**Examples:**

- Advising boards and executives on AI strategy and governance
- Being the voice your congregation, constituency or audience trusts on AI
- Acting as a trusted thinking partner for leaders navigating AI disruption
- Publishing trusted assessments of AI tools and developments for a specific industry

**Your question:** *Where is my ability to see clearly and advise well, most scarce and most valuable right now?*

*Changed:* the tagline (it was "Sell judgment, direction, and clarity"); "Best for" takes the Curator's "discernment" and "the reliability of your signal in a very noisy space"; "They guide organisations, leaders, and teams" became the people who look to them. *Added:* the last two sentences of "What this position does" (the Curator's work, as one way to advise) and the second and fourth examples. *Dropped:* "Guiding organisations through the human dimensions of AI adoption", which belongs to the Educator and the Convener now, and the Curator's three examples, which the fourth example and the new sentences carry.

### 4. Educator

*Build capability in others at scale*

**Best for:** Leaders who translate complex ideas into clear ones. Your advantage is the ability to meet people where they are, not where the technology is, and move them forward without leaving them behind.

**What this position does:** The Educator's product is understanding. They design and deliver learning experiences, frameworks, and programmes that help individuals, teams, and industries develop the literacy and confidence to operate in an AI-shaped world. Some build that capability in the people they already lead. Others take it to a whole industry. The leverage here is significant. One educator can change the trajectory of thousands.

**Examples:**

- Leading the AI learning of the people in your organisation, ministry or institution, so no one is left behind
- Designing AI literacy programmes for organisations or industries
- Creating frameworks that help non-technical leaders make AI decisions confidently
- Building curriculum, coaching, or content that shifts how people think about and work with AI

**Your question:** *Where is the capability gap in my industry or sphere of influence, and am I positioned to close it?*

*Added:* "Some build that capability in the people they already lead. Others take it to a whole industry." and the first example. Nothing else changed.

### 5. Convener (new)

*Bring people together around what no one can solve alone*

**Best for:** Leaders others will answer an invitation from: heads of communities, congregations, associations, networks and constituencies. Your advantage is trust across groups that do not otherwise work together, and the standing to rally people around an idea, even when you do not have the time to build it yourself.

**What this position does:** The Convener builds relationships and community around one friction that no single organisation can fix. They bring the right people to the table, keep them there, and turn a shared concern into shared action. They do not need to own the solution. They make it possible for others to build it, adopt it, and carry it further together.

**Examples:**

- Gathering the churches of a city to agree on a shared response to AI
- Running a joint AI readiness programme for the members of an association or network
- Bringing employers, schools and officials together around a workforce transition

**Your question:** *What friction in my world will only move if the right people move together, and would they come to the table for me?*

*New.* It takes the friction Day 3's Emerging zone already names: "Problems requiring more coordination than any single organisation could manage."

### 6. Guardian (new)

*Set the standards that let AI be trusted*

**Best for:** Leaders whose authority shapes how others operate: officials, regulators, board chairs, and the heads of religious bodies and institutions. Your advantage is the mandate to decide what is acceptable, and the trust that makes others follow it.

**What this position does:** The Guardian turns authority into the standards a whole institution, sector or community runs on: what AI may be used for, what must be disclosed, and what protections people are owed. Their product is trust. Good standards do not slow AI down. They are what lets it spread safely. Every leader governs their own use of AI. The Guardian sets the rules for many.

**Examples:**

- Issuing guidance on the use of AI in public services
- Setting the standard for AI use across a diocese, a denomination or a group of schools
- Adopting an organisation's AI policy, and the board oversight behind it
- Setting an institution's rules on AI in teaching, assessment or patient care

**Your question:** *Where do the people in my domain need standards they can trust before AI can spread safely, and do I hold the authority to set them?*

*New.* The line between the duty and the position: every stakeholder governs their own use of AI (Day 7); the Guardian makes governance their territory.

### Added after the six positions

> **Whatever your position, the standard for how AI is used in it is yours to set.** You write it on Day 7, in your Personal AI Policy.
