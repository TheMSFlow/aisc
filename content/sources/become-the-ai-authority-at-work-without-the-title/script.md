# Instagram script: Become the AI Authority at Work, Without the Title

**Briefing:** `become-the-ai-authority-at-work-without-the-title` · published 2026-09-05
**Cover:** Emerging Leader · "You Can Lead AI at Work Before Anyone Promotes You"
**Reframe:** Knowing the tools makes you the helpdesk. Bringing direction makes you the leader, and direction needs no title.
**395 words · about 2:40 at speaking pace**
**STOP form:** the question at your desk

Say it as written. Where a line fights your mouth, change it, but keep the length.

---

## STOP · 20 words

Someone leans over your desk and asks which AI app you use. You tell them. You just became the helpdesk.

## BELIEF · 58 words

And that feels like the right way to be useful. You found the tools early. You know which one drafts well and which one to avoid. People trust you with it. The leading part, the policy, what the team should actually do, that belongs to whoever gets the title. So you wait for someone to hand it over.

## TURN · 129 words

But look around. People around you are already using AI. Some openly, most quietly, no two the same way. What almost no team has is direction. Someone who can say what the tools are for, where they do not belong, and what good looks like.

Nobody owns that job yet. Titles go to jobs that already exist, and this one does not.

Take one task you already own. The weekly report, say. Run it deliberately, check everything, then write one page. What the tool does. What a person still checks. What it must never be used for.

Recommend apps and people ask you which app. Bring that page and they ask you what the team should do. That is leading, and you can do it before anyone promotes you.

## COST · 88 words

And the timing matters, because this job only gets filled once. Every organization will end up with someone who owns AI direction. Most have nobody yet.

So picture the quiet version. You fix the report. You sit with the colleague who is overwhelmed. You write the page. Then a planning meeting comes, someone louder says they would like to own AI, and nobody in the room knows you already do. The work you did goes to them. Not because they did it. Because they said it out loud.

## MOVE · 69 words

So this week, before you touch a tool, pick the task you want to claim and time it. Two or three real instances, written down, not remembered. Then run it the new way and time it again, with your checking included. That is a number nobody can take apart. The rest, including what to do when someone senior already owns this, is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 31 words

Nobody is going to ask you to lead this. The only real choice is whether the person who says it out loud is the one who did the work. Go first.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** A small, ordinary moment first, then the sting. Beat before "You just became the helpdesk", and say it dry. Read silently it works because the viewer has had that exact exchange this month.
- **STOP form is the question at your desk.** New form: a colleague's small request, answered helpfully, then the turn onto what answering it made you. Added to the catalogue in `SCRIPT_GUIDE.md`. Rows 15, 14 and 13 used the manager you remember, the private conclusion, and the pitch that already reached them, so none is in range.
- **The cover headline is "You Can Lead AI at Work Before Anyone Promotes You."** The TURN ends on that sentence almost verbatim, on purpose. Poster frame and payoff have to agree.
- **The opening pays itself off.** STOP makes the viewer the helpdesk; the last paragraph of TURN answers it ("Recommend apps and people ask you which app"). Both come from the briefing's callout, "The move is structure, not tools."
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.** It is a bridge: it says why the timing is load-bearing for the claim just made, then lets the cost land.
- **COST form is a thing someone else takes.** The scenario is the briefing's own ("skipping it is how the quiet builder watches someone louder get handed the ground they cleared"), set as a picture, not told as a real person's story. Do not attach a name or a cohort to it on camera.
- **No number in this script is invented.** It carries none. The briefing's worked figure (four hours down to ninety minutes, over six weeks) is deliberately left out of MOVE: it is an example of a sentence, and said aloud without that framing it would sound like a claim about the viewer's report.
- **The link is named once, in MOVE.** The close lands with no sign-off and no second CTA. AISC is not named: the briefing mentions the challenge in its close, but the question this video raises is answered by the briefing itself.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-become-the-ai-authority-at-work-without-the-title&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** "Go first." runs into "Someone leans over your desk" on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence at the end.
