## STOP · 12 words

Count the AI tools your company pays for. Now name what changed.

## BELIEF · 56 words

And that is how most people start. Someone mentions a tool at a dinner. Your team asks for a license for another one. A newsletter says the one you are using is already behind. So you collect. It feels like progress because the list keeps growing, and nobody has told you there is another way in.

## TURN · 126 words

But there are two ways to start with AI, and you can tell them apart inside a month.

The first asks what AI can do. That question never ends. The answer changes every month, none of it is about your work, and you can chase it forever without getting anywhere.

The second asks something narrower. Where, in my world specifically, has AI made something cheap that used to be expensive, and has anybody claimed that yet?

That question has an answer. It is specific to your seat. It does not change every month. And the answer is usually something you already suspected and never priced.

One of those questions makes you faster at work that was never the point. The other one changes where you stand.

## COST · 89 words

And the difference shows up faster than you would think.

Six months in, the tool collector is busier than before. A bit quicker at things that never mattered. No clearer about what any of it means for the business.

Meanwhile the opening in their world is still sitting there, and it is usually still open for one reason. Everybody assumes somebody better qualified is already moving on it.

In most industries that person does not exist. What everyone is waiting for is clarity, and clarity takes days, not years.

## MOVE · 59 words

So, one hour this week. Open a blank page and write three things. What has AI made cheap in your world that used to be expensive. What that makes rare and valuable. And who around you has claimed it yet.

Write it badly, then write it again. The full five-day version is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 32 words

You are done collecting tools. That question never ends. The other one has an answer, and it has been sitting in your world the whole time, waiting for somebody to name it.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** It is a count, not a scene, which is deliberate: row 1 opened on a scene and these should not all sound alike. Leave a beat before "Now name what changed."
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.** Only one in the script.
- **The link is named once, in MOVE, at the point the briefing is mentioned.** The close then lands the decision with no sign-off. This is a change from the earlier scripts, which referenced the briefing mid-script and only gave the path thirty seconds later, so the reference dangled.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-territory-not-tools-the-ai-opportunity-for-leaders&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** the close lands on "waiting for somebody to name it", which runs into "Count the AI tools your company pays for" on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence.
- The word "territory" never appears. It is the briefing's own term and it is right on the page, but spoken cold to someone who has not read it, it is jargon. The script says what it means instead.
- MOVE asks for three things in a row. Say them as three separate instructions with a pause after each, not as one sentence with commas.
