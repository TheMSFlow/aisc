# Instagram script: Your Congregation Is Already Asking

**Reframe:** They are being taught about this either way. The only open question is who is doing the teaching.

Say it as written. Where a line fights your mouth, change it, but keep the length.

## STOP · 17 words

Your people are asking about AI. In the car home, at the kitchen table. Just not you.

## BELIEF · 60 words

And it has not felt urgent, because it sits behind the building fund and the volunteer roster and the eleven other things actually on fire this week. It also feels like someone else's subject. You did not train for this. You're not going to stand up on Sunday and speak about a technology you would struggle to define.

## TURN · 111 words

Strip the technology away and this is not new. Every generation of ministry leadership has met something powerful that arrived in the lives of their people before it arrived in the teaching. Television. The internet. The smartphone. Social media.

And the ministries that pretended the new thing was not there did not protect anyone. Their people adopted it anyway, alone, without wisdom, and quietly learned that leadership had nothing to say about the thing reshaping their daily lives. The trust did not break loudly. It thinned.

Because the absence of teaching is itself a teaching. It says this does not concern us, while your people can plainly see that it does.

## COST · 94 words

And thinning trust is not the only thing running right now.

Every week without a word from leadership, hundreds of people in your congregation are quietly making up their own minds. Some fearful. Some reckless. Most of them alone.

Meanwhile someone on your staff has already pasted ministry information into a free tool nobody vetted, and they were not rebelling, they were solving a real capacity problem without direction.

And when the first visible incident lands, whatever you say next is damage control. What you say before it is leadership. Those are different speeches from different positions.

## MOVE · 61 words

So not a technology committee. Two quiet hours this week, and three plain questions. What do I actually believe AI is? What would faithful use look like here, and what would be off limits? And when a member asks me on Sunday, what do I say?

Write the answers down. What clarity costs and what it gives back is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 25 words

Do not leave the question going around unanswered. It will be answered by someone. The people you shepherd are hoping it will be you.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** "Just not you" is the whole hook. Leave a beat before it.
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST**, moving from thinning trust to what else is already running. Only one in the script.
- **The link is named once, in MOVE.** The close then lands with no sign-off, so the last words out of your mouth are "hoping it will be you" rather than a URL.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-your-congregation-is-already-asking-about-ai&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** the close lands on "hoping it will be you", which runs into "Your people are asking about AI" on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence rather than a sign-off.
- TURN lists four technologies as separate sentences. Say them slowly, with a pause between each: the list is doing the argument's work, and rushed it sounds like filler.
- This is the first faith-audience script in the queue. The register stays trusted-advisor, not devotional. No scripture, no ministry jargon, and never imply what the answer to the faithful-use question should be.
