## STOP · 15 words

Someone has pitched you an AI that works while you sleep. The capability is real.

## BELIEF · 52 words

And your instinct is the sensible one. You delegate to people all the time. A good deputy acts without asking you first, and you would not want one who checked every decision. So an agent sounds like the same arrangement, faster and cheaper, and you are comfortable with the principle already.

## TURN · 113 words

But the analogy breaks in three places, and each one matters.

A person knows when they are out of their depth. Mostly. And they feel the weight of your name on what they do. An agent has no such sense. It proceeds with exactly the same mechanical confidence inside and outside the situations you thought of.

A person learns your standards from a hundred small corrections. An agent holds exactly the standards you wrote down. No more. And it inherits every ambiguity in them.

And when a person makes a mistake, they usually make it once. An agent runs its mistake at machine speed until something stops it. Fifty wrong emails, not one.

## COST · 95 words

And none of that is an argument against agents.

It just moves where the leadership work sits. You stop doing the tasks and you start designing the boundaries. In practice a boundary is a short, unglamorous document that says five things. What the agent may do without a human. What information it may touch. What forces it to stop and ask. How often somebody reviews what it did while nobody was watching. And who can switch it off.

Nothing on that list is technical. All of it is instruction you have written before, for people.

## MOVE · 70 words

So when the proposal reaches your desk, five questions. What exactly can it do without a human, as a list, not a category. Where are the boundaries written and who owns them. What does it escalate, and how would we know if it failed to. What is the blast radius of its worst hour. Who is accountable, by name. The rest is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 34 words

An agent is your judgment, running unattended. If the judgment has not been built yet, that is what runs unattended. So before anyone hands over keys, make sure the thing being scaled actually exists.
