## STOP · 13 words

Someone asked if AI could write the performance reviews. You are still thinking.

## BELIEF · 63 words

And you cannot answer it, because the honest answer is you are not sure where the line is. Nobody drew you one. You have picked things up in passing, from a colleague who swears by it, from whatever your team has started doing without asking. So each request gets decided on instinct, one at a time, and you hope the instinct is right.

## TURN · 124 words

But you do not need to know what AI can do to answer that. You need two questions about the task, not the technology.

First. How much of this follows a pattern? Structured, repeatable, the same shape every time. A monthly report that pulls the same numbers into the same format is nearly all pattern. A conversation with someone who is quietly thinking about leaving has none.

Second. How much of this needs something only a person has? Judgment. Trust built over years. Reading what is not being said in a room.

Ask both of any task and it lands in one of four piles. And performance reviews, since you asked, are high on both. Which is exactly why they are the hardest ones.

## COST · 98 words

And one of those four is where the money is.

Everyone finds the easy two. Pattern work goes, judgment stays. The one that gets missed is the work that scores high on both. Strategic planning. Board papers. The analysis under a pricing decision.

That pile does not go to AI and it does not stay away from it. It splits. AI does the gathering and the drafting and argues the other side. You do the deciding.

Miss that and you have automated your admin while the decisions that actually matter stayed as under-prepared as they have always been.

## MOVE · 69 words

So, one sitting this week. Open last week's calendar, list what you actually did, and run both questions over every line.

Then act on one from each pile. Hand one pattern task over with standards attached. Put one judgment task back. Split one real decision. And the fourth pile, low on both, do not automate it. Delete it. The full sort is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 29 words

Time was never the constraint. Everyone gets the same amount. Judgment is the constraint, and delegating well is just refusing to spend judgment on work that only needs attention.
