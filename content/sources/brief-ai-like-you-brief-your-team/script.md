## STOP · 19 words

You once worked for a manager who said, just take a pass at this. Now you are that manager.

## BELIEF · 57 words

Because you did try one of these tools. Probably after somebody at dinner would not stop talking about it. You typed a line, maybe eight words, and read what came back. It was fine. Generic, a little bland, nothing you would put your name on. And you closed it thinking, this is not built for my work.

## TURN · 123 words

Here is what nobody tells you. You were not using a search box badly. You were briefing a new hire badly.

Look at what the thing actually is. Fast, tireless, read more than anyone you will ever hire, and brand new to you. It knows how the world generally does things. It knows nothing about your audience, your standards, your situation, your voice, unless you say so.

So brief it the way you brief a person. Who to be. What the situation is. What done looks like.

Act as a chief of staff writing for executives who read fast. I run a forty person division and we missed our target this month. One page, three sections, under four hundred words, no corporate cheer.

## COST · 96 words

Now, that takes longer to type than what you have been typing, and that gap is the whole argument.

The brief I just gave you takes ninety seconds. Thirty if you dictate it. The version most people send takes eight. Eight seconds feels like the efficient choice, and it buys you three rounds of disappointing drafts and nothing you would send.

Because vagueness does not spare the tool a single decision. Who is speaking, who is listening, what tone this calls for, when to stop. It just hands them over. Made badly, quietly, on your behalf.

## MOVE · 69 words

So this week, one task. Not your workflow. One task, something you write every month. Give it the three sentences. When the first draft misses, do not start over, redirect it like a person. Keep the structure, make the opening direct. Then check the facts before it leaves your hands, and save the brief that worked. The whole brief, written out, is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 33 words

The question was never whether the tool is good enough. It is whether you brief it like the manager you admired, or the one you used to complain about. You know the difference.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** It is a memory first, an accusation second. Beat before "Now you are that manager", and land it dry, not sharp. Read silently it works because everyone has that manager.
- **STOP form is the manager you remember.** New form: a person from their past, then the turn onto them. Add it to the catalogue in `SCRIPT_GUIDE.md`. Row 14 used the private conclusion and row 13 the pitch that already reached them, so neither is in range.
- **The cover headline is "AI Is Not a Search Box. It Is a New Hire."** The TURN says that sentence almost verbatim in its second line, on purpose. Poster frame and first line have to agree.
- **The example brief in the TURN is the load-bearing thirty seconds.** Say it at normal pace, not fast, and do not paraphrase it live. A viewer who hears an actual brief said out loud gets the whole skill; one who hears it described gets nothing.
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.** It is a bridge, not a tease: it concedes the obvious objection before making the cost land.
- **Memory is deliberately absent.** The briefing's "where the analogy breaks" section notes that AI largely starts cold, that some tools have added memory features as of mid-2026, and that the behaviour is uneven across products. That caveat needs its full date anchor to be said honestly, which costs around twenty-five words the script does not have, so nothing in the spoken copy claims anything about what AI does or does not remember. Nothing here implies the tool accumulates context the way a hire does, and no line says it never does either. If Michael wants it in, it belongs at the end of TURN and must carry the mid-2026 anchor exactly.
- **The link is named once, in MOVE.** The close lands with no sign-off and no second CTA. AISC is not named: the question this briefing raises is answered by the briefing itself.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-brief-ai-like-you-brief-your-team&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** "the one you used to complain about" runs straight back into the STOP on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence at the end.
- No number in this script is invented. Ninety seconds, thirty dictated, eight seconds, and three rounds of drafts are all the briefing's own, as is the forty person division in the example.
