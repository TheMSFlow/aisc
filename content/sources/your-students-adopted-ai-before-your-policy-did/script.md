## STOP · 18 words

Third period, AI is cheating. Fourth period, the teacher builds the lesson with it. Same student, same building.

## BELIEF · 62 words

And from your seat that looks like a staffing question. Two teachers with different views, which is normal, and a policy that will settle it when it comes. You have a committee, or you are on one. There is a district office above you. You did not create this gap and you cannot close it with a budget you do not control.

## TURN · 116 words

Except that student is learning something in the gap between those two rooms, and it is not on anybody's curriculum. They are learning to read which adult is in front of them and adjust. That is a real skill. It is just not the one you meant to teach.

And two years of institutional energy went into one question. Are they cheating with it. Real question, and it ate the agenda while the bigger ones sat untouched. What does honest work look like when drafting is nearly free. What are we actually assessing.

An institution that only knows how to detect does not have a position on AI. It has an anxiety with a tool attached.

## COST · 93 words

And the ban does not fix it. It moves it.

Prohibition felt decisive in the first semester and was unenforceable by the second. Usage did not stop, it went somewhere you cannot see. Which means a school that bans AI does not produce students who do not use AI. It produces students who use it with no adult anywhere near them.

Meanwhile someone on staff is pasting student names, learning difficulties and family circumstances into a free tool, solving a real workload problem with no direction. In education that data belongs to children.

## MOVE · 67 words

So, three lines this month. They need no budget and no committee. Student data never goes into unapproved tools. Nothing AI-drafted reaches a family without a named adult who read it. Assessment decisions are made by educators.

Three lines you can enforce beat thirty pages you cannot. The fourth move, the one that changes your standing in the building, is in the briefing, linked in my bio.

## CLOSE · 32 words

Every other sector is managing money or output or reputation. You are forming the people who will live longest inside whatever this turns into. That student in third period is not waiting.

## Production notes

- **This script enters from the student's side, and that is the whole reason it works.** Every other script in the set opens on the leader's own situation. This one puts a principal or dean inside their building as a fifteen-year-old experiences it, for about eight seconds, before handing them their own seat back in BELIEF. Do not "fix" it back to a leader-first opening.
- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** Two rooms, then the four-word sting. Beat before "Same student, same building."
- **The close returns to that same student.** "That student in third period" is a deliberate callback and it is what makes the ending land rather than summarise. Do not generalise it to "students".
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.** Only one in the script.
- **The link is named once, in MOVE.** The close lands with no sign-off.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-your-students-adopted-ai-before-your-policy-did&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** the close lands on "is not waiting", which runs into "Third period, AI is cheating" on a rewatch. This one loops better than any other script in the set. Leave a full beat of silence.
- TURN carries two questions said as statements. Do not let the pitch rise: they are the questions nobody asked, being listed, not asked now.
- Register is peer to peer with someone accountable upward and directing downward. Never imply they should have acted sooner, and never suggest going around the district or the board.
