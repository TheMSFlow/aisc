## STOP · 15 words

Think back to Friday. Could you say where the week went, with a straight face?

## BELIEF · 60 words

And the week was not empty. It was full. Reading, sorting, checking, compiling, reformatting, chasing status. Every one of those felt necessary while you were doing it. The calendar says you were busy, and you were. That is the part that makes this hard to see, because busy and productive have felt like the same word your entire career.

## TURN · 121 words

But two different kinds of work were wearing the same clothes.

Judgment work needs discernment. Trade-offs, real consequences, calls that cannot be repeated safely without context. Deciding what not to pursue. Making a decision with half the information. This kind is finite. It runs out.

Attention work needs focus, not judgment. Repeatable, pattern-driven, predictable output. Reading and sorting. Compiling. Monitoring. Rewriting the same explanation again. This kind expands. It takes whatever you give it, and it does not get better because you are senior.

And attention work is loud. It feels urgent because it is noisy, not because it matters. Here is the test. If delaying it a day would change nothing, it is attention work wearing urgency as a costume.

## COST · 100 words

And here is what that costs you twice.

Run the audit and most leaders find ten to twenty hours a week of attention work sitting in their own calendar, in their own handwriting. Price it at what your time costs the organization and the annual figure lands somewhere between sobering and absurd.

But that arithmetic only prices the hours. It does not price what the hours were taken from. The thinking that kept getting deferred. The pattern you would have caught early. The decision that waited so long it made itself.

If everything needs your attention, nothing gets your judgment.

## MOVE · 70 words

So, one sitting this week. Pull up last week's calendar, list what you did, and ask one question of every line. If a capable person had clear instructions, could they do eighty percent of this?

Every yes is an hour that belongs in your count. Then decide what those hours buy before you recover them, or the same work grows back inside a month. The briefing is in my bio.

## CLOSE · 29 words

Your margin is not a productivity trick. It is the space where decisions get considered instead of reacted to. Treat a number that size like the asset it is.
