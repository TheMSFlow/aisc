## STOP · 17 words

Somebody in your building pasted a client document into a chatbot this week. Nobody will tell you.

## BELIEF · 60 words

And if someone asked where you stand on AI, you would probably say you are still looking at it. Somebody owns a workstream. There is a committee, or there will be. When the time comes you will get proper advice, write something careful, and send it round. That is how you have handled every serious decision you have ever made.

## TURN · 119 words

But the looking-at-it phase ended a while ago. Nobody called a meeting about it.

Somebody summarized a confidential file with a free tool because it saved them an hour. Somebody sent a client something an AI wrote and nobody checked it. Somebody made a call based on an answer they never verified, and it went out with your name on it.

None of that is written down anywhere. But every one of those choices set a rule, and the rules people actually follow are the policy. Not the document. The habit.

Ask three people on three floors what the rules are. If you get three answers, that is not a problem coming. That is the problem, running right now.

## COST · 89 words

And that matters because of who answers for it.

When the first incident goes public, and one will, the coverage will not name the person who pasted the document. It will not name the tool. Boards ask one question and they ask it in one direction. Who was leading this.

And you will not have one ready, because nobody ever decided.

You will be answering for rules you never wrote and cannot show anyone. That is not a technology problem. It is a leadership problem wearing a technology costume.

## MOVE · 69 words

So, this week. Ask your people how they are actually using AI, and make it clear nobody is in trouble. They will keep it quiet as long as the official line is that you are still looking at it.

What they tell you will be more than you expect, and that is where your real rules should come from. The briefing has the four questions those rules must answer.

## CLOSE · 36 words

The risk is already inside your organization. So is the authority to direct it. Right now both of them belong to whoever is quietly experimenting. Not to you.

Full briefing at the link in my bio.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** Scene first, then the sting. Leave a beat before "Nobody will tell you" and say it flat.
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.** Only one in the script.
- **The native embed is inside MOVE**, one sentence. Do not add a second CTA.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-ai-governance-the-risk-is-already-inside&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** the close lands on "Not to you", which runs into "Somebody in your building" on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence rather than a sign-off.
- TURN has three sentences starting "Somebody". That repetition is the engine of the beat. Do not vary the openings to make it sound less repetitive; the sameness is what makes it feel like it is happening everywhere at once.
- "Not the document. The habit." is the hinge of the whole script. Full stop before each. Do not run them together.
