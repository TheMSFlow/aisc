## STOP · 18 words

You have quietly decided you are just not a disciplined person. Wrong diagnosis. It is not you.

## BELIEF · 58 words

Because you know the week. It starts with a plan and ends with a blur. The loudest thing won every day, decisions got made at the speed of the inbox, the strategic work slid to Friday and then to never, and you went home each evening having worked flat out on nothing you actually chose.

## TURN · 118 words

But here is the mechanism nobody explains to a new manager.

Two kinds of work compete for your day. Judgment work needs discernment. Real trade-offs, real consequences, and it depletes you to do. Attention work just needs focus. Reading, sorting, checking, re-explaining, being the final pass on things that never needed you.

And when attention work fills the day, judgment does not get saved for the moments that need it. It gets pre-spent.

So the genuinely consequential question arrives at three in the afternoon, and the discernment it deserved was already burned on an inbox and two status meetings. The big call gets made reactively. Not because you lack judgment. Because it was gone.

## COST · 94 words

And the loop feeds itself.

Rushed decisions create rework. Rework creates fires. Fires eat more of the margin. Run that for a quarter and you become the reactive one, the way a flooded engine becomes slow. The flaw was never in the driver.

Which is why the personal-discipline cures keep failing. The earlier alarm, the time-management book, the guilt. You cannot alarm-clock your way out of a position that manufactures attention work faster than you can process it. Your seat is where the organization's attention work concentrates. That is structural, not personal.

## MOVE · 70 words

So, one thing changed. Now the attention layer can be delegated without hiring anyone, because AI absorbs patterned work under direction. Not your judgment. Nothing makes your trade-offs or has the conversation your struggling report needs.

Then protect what you reclaim, and tell the person above you what those hours are producing. Framed as availability it gets overruled. Framed as output it gets defended. The briefing is in my bio.

## CLOSE · 32 words

A few weeks in, something strange happens. The firefighter identity dissolves, because it was a description of a condition and not a person. You were never the fire. You were standing in one.

## Production notes

- **The STOP line is the on-screen caption.** It names a private thought most managers have never said aloud, then contradicts it in four words. Beat before "It is not you", and say it warmly. This is the only script in the set whose opening is reassurance rather than provocation.
- **STOP form is the private conclusion.** New form. Add it to the catalogue in `SCRIPT_GUIDE.md`.
- **This is the second Leaders of Leaders script and the tone is different from row 6.** That one was about agency. This one is about absolution first, agency second. Do not let it become a pep talk, but do not strip the warmth out of it either.
- **"It gets pre-spent" is the hinge of the whole script.** Full stop before it. Say those three words slowly and let them sit.
- **The re-hook is the first line of COST.**
- **The link is named once, in MOVE.** The close lands with no sign-off.
- **Bio link** carries `utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=briefing-reactive-leadership-is-a-margin-problem&utm_content=video`, set on `michaelsteve.com/links` for the run of this post.
- **Loop check:** the close lands on "You were standing in one", which runs into "You have quietly decided you are just not a disciplined person" on a rewatch. Leave a beat of silence.
- The briefing's closing line is "You were never the fire. You were standing in one, and there is a door." The script drops the door clause on purpose. Spoken, it over-resolves; the shorter version leaves the viewer with the reframe rather than a promise.
- AI is named once, in MOVE, and only as the thing that makes the attention layer delegable. Do not add more. The piece earns its AI point by spending two minutes on a problem the viewer already feels.
